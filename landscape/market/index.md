# Market and competitive analysis

> **Status: a survey, as of 24 Oct 2026.** What exists in the market that does part of what SHarness is designed to do, who makes it, how it enforces, and what it leaves out; where SHarness sits; what it should reuse; and the reasons the market might not need it. Researched by sharness.build on 24 October 2026 from the sources listed on each row, every one read that day. *Not verified* marks a claim that appeared only in a secondary or vendor source, or whose primary page could not be read. Five load-bearing rows were re-checked against their primary pages by a second pass (§8). The editorial responsibility is Dinis Cruz's; nothing here is advice to buy or sell anything.

## 1. The market in one paragraph

The buyer is whoever lets an agent run commands, read files, call tools and hold credentials on their behalf: a developer using Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor or Antigravity; a platform team running agent fleets on Kubernetes or in cloud sandboxes; an enterprise security team that now carries agents on its risk register. The reason it is a market now is that prompt injection is unsolved and officially named as the central threat: the May 2026 guidance from the Five Eyes agencies, co-signed by the UK NCSC, calls it "the most persistent and difficult-to-fix threat" and says "no single control is sufficient" ([CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-cisa-agentic-ai-guidance), secondary); Simon Willison's "lethal trifecta" (private data, untrusted content, a way to communicate out) argues that a 95 percent detection rate "is very much a failing grade" ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jun/16/the-lethal-trifecta/)). Between late 2025 and late 2026 the industry moved visibly from classifier guardrails towards enforcement the model cannot talk its way past: OS sandboxes on by default in Codex and Cursor; Anthropic open-sourcing its bubblewrap and Seatbelt runtime (October 2025); Docker, Fly.io, Cloudflare, Vercel and Microsoft launching microVM or isolate sandboxes for agents (January to April 2026); NVIDIA's OpenShell keeping provider credentials outside the sandbox; and credential brokering (the agent sees a placeholder, a proxy swaps in the real key) appearing in Claude Code, Docker Sandboxes, Cloudflare, 1Password, Infisical and several small open-source tools. Money followed: Zenity ($125M, August 2026), Obsidian ($85M at $1.1B, August 2026), HiddenLayer ($100M, September 2026), Oasis ($120M, March 2026), Straiker ($64M, June 2026), and acquisitions of Lakera, Prompt Security, Aim, Protect AI, CalypsoAI, Portkey and Astrix (§6). Gartner's framing, "guardian agents" taking "at least 10 to 15%" of the agentic AI market by 2030, is the analyst number most quoted ([Gartner, 11 Jun 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-11-gartner-predicts-that-guardian-agents-will-capture-10-15-percent-of-the-agentic-ai-market-by-2030)). SHarness enters a market that has accepted its premise, kernel enforcement and secrets outside the agent, and is now arguing about granularity, portability, and who runs the launcher.

## 9. Where SHarness sits

**Not a sandbox product and not a guardrail: a process architecture.** Everything in §2 draws one boundary around the whole agent and leaves the agent, its tools, its credentials and its network client together inside it. SHarness's distinctive claims, all designed and none built:

1. **Per-component kernel least privilege.** Single-purpose processes (launcher, kms, net, fs, bash, llm), each with its own ephemeral uid, Landlock ruleset, seccomp allowlist, namespaces, `no_new_privs` and cgroup limits, talking only over a deny-by-default mutual TLS edge matrix issued by a session CA whose key is destroyed after issuing. The nearest analogues are OpenShell (one sandbox, not several) and Wassette (per-tool deny by default, in Wasm rather than the kernel).
2. **A KMS that never returns a clear key.** Keys sealed to one-time keys; the private key in a frozen process in a `memfd_secret` page; an empty network namespace; no filesystem rights after start. Nobody in this survey does this for agent credentials. The market's current best practice is "inject at the proxy so the agent sees a sentinel" (Claude Code's mask, Docker Sandboxes, Cloudflare, Infisical, Cloak), which still puts the clear key in a proxy process the agent can reach over loopback.
3. **A policy hash supplied from outside the agent**, layers composed by intersection, so a prompt-injected agent cannot widen its own grant. Claude Code's managed settings and Antigravity's presets are the commercial equivalents, and both are settings read by the agent process itself: a *setting* in [ABP's terms](https://riskmandate.ai/abp.html), not a *boundary*.
4. **The LLM gateway as a locked component** with a secret filter on tool results, Messages API compatible. LiteLLM, Portkey and Cloudflare AI Gateway hold keys and apply guardrails as ordinary services with full OS privileges.
5. **Transparent versus harness-aware apps.** An unmodified app gets a folder from `sharness.fs`, `HTTPS_PROXY` to `sharness.net`, a model endpoint at `sharness.llm`, a shell via `sharness.bash`; only secret handling needs code changes.

**Reuse rather than rebuild.** Landlock bindings and CLI patterns from landrun; bubblewrap as the namespace primitive, and the proxy, allowlist and credential-mask semantics from Anthropic's sandbox-runtime; Cedar for a policy language and for static analysis of grants, as ToolHive does; SPIFFE SVID formats for the per-component certificates, so identity products can consume them; OpenTelemetry and OCSF trace shapes from OWASP's Agent Control Standard, so platforms like Zenity can ingest SHarness events; Wassette for tools that compile to Wasm; Firecracker, Docker Sandboxes or Agent Sandbox as the outer boundary in topologies where the launcher runs outside the agent. The measured 3.2 ms per mutual TLS handshake and 49 ms per ephemeral user ([Evidence](../../evidence/index.md)) say the per-component design is affordable.

**Direct competitors** (same job, same buyer): NVIDIA OpenShell and NemoClaw (closest in keeping credentials off the agent); Anthropic's sandbox-runtime with credential masking and managed settings (the default path for Claude Code users); Codex CLI's Landlock and seccomp sandbox; Antigravity's sandbox; Docker Sandboxes (credentials injected outside the microVM, central policy); Cloudflare Dynamic Workers (credential injection at egress); microsandbox.

**Complements** (SHarness could be their enforcement layer): the platforms in §7, which monitor and block at API hooks and would gain a kernel-enforced boundary to point at; identity products (Okta, Entra Agent ID, Keycard, Oasis, Teleport), which issue identity but do not confine; secrets managers (1Password's broker, Infisical, Vault, AgentCore Identity) as the upstream for `sharness.kms`; LLM gateways, which could sit behind `sharness.llm`; MCP gateways (ToolHive, Docker's), whose servers could run as harnessed apps; and ABP, which measures grants and would use SHarness as the control that turns an *expectation* into a *boundary*.

**Honest gaps.** As of 28 Oct 2026 SHarness is experiments and three MVPs, released to PyPI as v0.2.0 on 29 Oct 2026, while the competitors ship today with daily users. SHarness is Linux-only by construction (Landlock 5.13+, TCP rules in newer kernels, `memfd_secret`), while buyers' desktops are often macOS and Windows, which Anthropic, OpenAI and Google cover with Seatbelt and AppContainer. Landlock and unprivileged user namespaces are often unavailable inside CI containers and hardened distributions. Root and kernel compromise are out of scope, whereas microVM vendors sell a hardware boundary. There is no cloud offering, no user interface, no team, no compliance story (SOC 2, ISO 42001 mapping, ACS traces), no CVE process. Several processes, a session CA and a KMS protocol are more surface than one bubblewrap profile. And the site's own caveat decides the value: "who runs the launcher decides what the locks are" ([Components](../../design/components/index.md#sharness-launcher)).

## 11. Threats to the thesis

1. **Platforms are building it in.** Anthropic ships credential masking, domain allowlists and admin-locked sandbox settings; Docker injects credentials outside the microVM under central policy; Cloudflare injects at egress; NVIDIA keeps provider credentials outside OpenShell. A developer running Claude Code inside Docker Sandboxes with masked credentials gets much of SHarness's practical benefit today, with two vendors' support. SHarness has to show that the residual gap (a clear key reachable in the proxy, one uid for agent and tools, settings rather than boundaries) is one people will act on.
2. **Hardware boundaries may be "good enough".** The market has largely settled on a microVM per session. If the blast radius of a prompt-injected agent is one disposable VM with scoped credentials, fine-grained least privilege inside it may look like diminishing returns.
3. **Confidential computing absorbs the budget.** TEEs answer a different question (trust in the operator) but give buyers a "secure AI" story that does not involve process confinement.
4. **Agents move server-side.** Claude Code on the web, Foundry hosted agents, AgentCore, Codex cloud: when the vendor runs the sandbox and holds the credentials, the vendor has answered "who runs the launcher" and a local harness has no role. The design's [topology E](../../design/architecture/index.md#deployment-topologies) is the same answer from the other side.
5. **Standards converge on identity and traces**, not process architecture. Procurement checklists may never ask for Landlock.
6. **Portability.** Linux-only kernel features exclude macOS and Windows desktops and many CI containers; the vendors who chose Seatbelt and AppContainer did so for reach.
7. **Complexity and trust.** With no code, no audits and no published threat-model document, early adopters have nothing to verify. The honest status labels are a strength for credibility and a weakness for adoption until the first component ships.
